Toy Product Design

MIT 2.00b | Spring 2017

Project: Navigate the design process to protoype novel toy products
Course: MIT 2.00b (Toy Product Design). An introduction to the product design process with a focus on designing for play and entertainment. Topics include brainstorming; estimation; sketching; graphic design; drawing and marker rendering; sketch modeling; concept development; design aesthetics; prototyping; and written, visual, and oral communication.
Collaborators: Dorothy Szymkiewicz, Samer Awale, Fernando Herrera

Brainstorming

After exploring a few potential concepts, our team decided to pursue a two-player riff on the classic whack-a-mole game, which we called Whack. The game was simple, two players (red vs. blue) would race to convert a shared set of twelve targets to their color before their opponent.

Playtesting at the Boston Children's Museum

Our first sketch model was a foam core prototype demonstrating the actuation and nature of gameplay. We took our prototype to the Boston Children's Museum for some valuable real-life testing and feedback from lots of eager helpers.

Second Prototype

Our next prototype featured a more realistic early version of our physical targets, coupled with some simple electronics including an LED strip to indicated when a target had been hit. We took this for another round of playtesting before deciding on our final design.

Key Contributions

In addition to ideation and rapid prototyping efforts, I was personally tasked with designing the electronics to be embedded in the final product, as well as writing and testing Arduino code to control the full system.

Final Presentation

Our work culminated in a live-audience presentation of our final product, which we of course opted to do in the form a Shakespearean skit.
